# Memetic levels for ideas generally

Research conducted 2026-09-26 Pacific / 2026-09-27 UTC. The user explicitly
broadened the scope to ideas in general. This is a research synthesis and draft
scale. It does not change the active rubric or reinterpret historical ratings.

The search found relevant taxonomies of selection criteria, transmission stages,
message properties, adoption, and shared practice. It did not establish a
validated universal ladder for the particular combination of expressive appeal,
memorability, generativity and cultural identity sought here. The proposed levels
below are our synthesis; none of these authors proposes this exact scale.

## Closest existing frameworks

### Memetic selection: assimilation, retention, expression, transmission

Chielens and Heylighen describe a four-stage replication model and candidate
selection criteria, including novelty, coherence, utility, simplicity and ease
of expression. Their paper proposes empirical methods such as transmission
chains. This is directly about ideas generally, but it is a theoretical model
and research program rather than an established aesthetic grading scale.
It usefully separates getting an idea into a mind from retaining and passing it
on. [Primary paper](https://pespmc1.vub.ac.be/Papers/MemeOperationalization.pdf).

### Idea stickiness: the Heath brothers' SUCCESs framework

Their practical framework identifies simplicity, unexpectedness, concreteness,
credibility, emotion and stories. These are message-design attributes, not
ordered degrees of quality. It helps describe a portable core and the devices
that make someone care about it. It does not establish that the most memorable
idea is the most generative or accurate. [Authors' framework](https://heathbrothers.com/wp-content/uploads/resources/mts-made-to-stick-model.pdf).

### Sharing: Berger's STEPPS framework

Berger distinguishes social currency, triggers, emotion, public visibility,
practical value and stories. This adds a reason to share: an idea may help
someone connect, contribute, or express identity. The workbook is a practical
synthesis. Berger and Milkman's separate empirical work examines online sharing;
its outcomes should not be relabeled as long-term cultural depth.
[Author's workbook](https://jonahberger.com/wp-content/uploads/2013/03/Crafting-Contagious-Workbook.pdf),
[research paper](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=1528077).

### Adoption: Rogers' diffusion framework

Rogers treats perceived advantage, compatibility, complexity, trialability and
observability as attributes affecting adoption. This gives us a separate question:
can someone try the idea and see what it enables? Adoption also depends on the
audience and social setting. The attributes are not rungs from weak to strong
memetic identity. [Author's book excerpt at the publisher](https://www.simonandschuster.com/books/Diffusion-of-Innovations-5th-Edition/Everett-M-Rogers/9780743222099).

### Imitation and variation: Shifman's content, form and stance

Shifman distinguishes the proposition, its perceptible expression, and the
communicative position people take toward it. These can be copied or changed
independently. Her study concerns digital culture; extending it to all ideas
is our inference. It gives us a precise way to ask what survives a variation:
the claim, the recognizable pattern, or a way of addressing other people.
[Primary article](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/jcc4.12013).

### Shared grammar: communities of practice

Etienne and Beverly Wenger-Trayner describe communities through a shared domain,
relationships, and practice. A developing repertoire includes stories, tools
and ways to address recurring problems. This is a useful analogue for the
desired insider fluency. It is an account of sustained social learning; a
single memorable phrase cannot demonstrate such a community by itself.
[Authors' introduction, reproduced by the University of Arkansas](https://uark.pressbooks.pub/edtech/chapter/introduction-to-communities-of-practice-wenger-trayner/).

Blackmore's related term **memeplex** describes a group of memes that propagate
more effectively together than separately. It offers a theoretical vocabulary
for mutually supporting ideas, rather than another quality level. Our proposed
top level concerns a shared repertoire; that does not establish Blackmore's
broader claims about consciousness or require accepting them.
[Author's account](https://www.susanblackmore.uk/conferences/dismantling-the-selfplex-meme-machines-and-the-nature-of-consciousness/).

Two adjacent results help constrain the interpretation. Hekkert and colleagues
found joint contributions of novelty and typicality to preference in product
design; novelty alone is an inadequate proxy for appeal. Norenzayan and
colleagues found a delayed-memory advantage for mostly intuitive material with
a minority of counterintuitive concepts in their narrative studies. Neither
result establishes a universal optimum for arbitrary ideas.
[Design study](https://doi.org/10.1348/000712603762842147),
[narrative study](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/epdf/10.1207/s15516709cog0000_68).

Some search results literally use memetic levels. Spiral Dynamics, for example,
uses vMemes to classify value systems. That is a different classification
problem from the memorability, appeal or generative capacity of an individual
idea; its levels should not be imported into this rubric.
[Framework's own account](https://www.spiral-dynamics.com/resources_colors_sd.htm).

## Proposed levels

This is a provisional vocabulary for increasing kinds of influence. It is not
a demonstrated developmental sequence or a claim that all ideas belong on one
strictly ordered axis. The intended audience and the idea's unit must be named.

| Level | Name | Distinct capacity | Evidence that would support it |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 0 | Transient | The explanation leaves little portable identity or organizing core. | After exposure, readers cannot reconstruct a distinctive central idea. |
| 1 | Catchy | A striking image, contrast, phrase or turn attracts attention. | Readers notice and recognize the hook; durable understanding is not yet established. |
| 2 | Sticky | A coherent core survives memory and retelling. The hook carries meaning. | Delayed recall and another person's retelling preserve the important relationship. |
| 3 | Generative | The idea offers an appealing way to think or act. People can extend it to new situations while preserving its recognizable logic. | Readers produce sound new applications or variations and want to keep using the perspective. |
| 4 | Cultural | The idea participates in a shared vocabulary, repertoire and practice that others can inhabit and extend. | Independent participants sustain recognizable variations, references and uses over time. |

The proposed level-3 target preserves the appetite for more: the reader wants
to think with the idea and make further things from it. Merely adding a special
name does not establish that capacity. Level 4 identifies social realization
and belongs to a family or practice; a single artifact can supply evidence of
its contribution, or a hypothesis of potential, but cannot demonstrate an
entire culture from its wording alone.

For an illustrative distinction, remembering an aphorism supplies evidence for
stickiness. Applying its organizing relationship to a genuinely new problem
adds evidence for generativity. A community developing linked concepts,
examples and practices around that relationship adds different, social evidence.
These are proposed assessment distinctions, not measured classifications of
particular famous ideas.

## Keep a profile beside any headline level

Record attraction, retention, transmission, generativity and shared identity
separately. An item can be widely forwarded while offering little reusable
insight; a specialist idea can support many applications within a small audience.
A single average would conceal those differences. Truth, ethical value,
correctness and practical benefit also need their own assessments.

Each judgment should distinguish **predicted potential** from **observed
response**. An artifact-only model can offer a reading prediction and identify
the concrete mechanism behind it. Claims about recall, voluntary sharing or
community adoption need corresponding human evidence. Unavailable reception
evidence is unknown, not proof of level 0. No existing score distribution
should be relabeled as the output of these new definitions.

## Calibration before deployment

1. Choose fresh examples from explanations, aphorisms, scientific concepts,
   social ideas and practical heuristics. State the audience and exposure.
   Avoid using the failed code pilot as the calibration target.
2. Record human reading preferences before model review. Preserve disagreements
   between immediate appeal, accurate understanding and desire to reuse.
3. Include controls for an attractive but empty slogan, decorative renaming,
   a useful but unremarkable statement, an inviting reusable lens, and a family
   whose shared vocabulary actually supports new examples.
4. Test the boundaries directly: immediate recognition, delayed reconstruction,
   retelling, and application to an unseen case. Any specific delay or sample
   size chosen for a pilot must be recorded as a design choice, not as a
   standard supplied by the literature above.
5. Reserve held-out examples and evaluate model agreement with the recorded
   preferences. Report uncertainty and audience dependence. Change the live
   rubric only as a new version with new evidence; preserve historical receipts.

No reader experiment or new model calibration was conducted in this research
increment. This increment does not modify `perch-style.json`; its existing
memetic definitions and historical receipts remain in force.
